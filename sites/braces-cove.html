Where they stood

Lane's source drawing is inscribed “Brace's Rock, Eastern Point / F.H. Lane del. / Aug. 1863” and lists five buyers for paintings made from it. The catalogue says he drew from “the coast just north of the cove,” not the beach.176 The marker is the nearest shore point north of the cove (MassGIS); the exact spot is not recorded. Duveneck painted the same rock about 1893.8 From the marker the rock lies south-southeast; because the Terra Foundation describes Lane's view as looking east across the cove, he may have stood farther west along the shore.

The light

The Terra Foundation describes the view as looking east across the cove, “the unseen sun in the west” tinting the far rocks pink while the foreground lies in shadow: late afternoon.177 One version shows autumn foliage, so Lane varied the season.177

Paint it today

  • In Lane's day the cove was part of the private Niles Farm, with Niles Pond between it and the harbor.176 We could not verify public shore access today.
  • Go late in the afternoon with the sun behind you, and watch the far rocks turn warm in the last hour.
  • Lane worked from a measured drawing, then painted several near-identical canvases from it. Make the drawing on site and the painting at home.
