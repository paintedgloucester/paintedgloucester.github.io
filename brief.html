The short answer

What the record shows

Winslow Homer and Childe Hassam are the only painters on the original list with verified paintings of Gloucester in public collections. Homer's two summers, in 1873 and 1880, produced oils now at the National Gallery of Art and the Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art, and watercolors at the Met, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, the Philadelphia Museum of Art, Princeton and elsewhere.1 Hassam's harbor views of about 1899 are at the Norton Museum of Art and the Newark Museum, and his 1918–19 pictures at the Met, PAFA and the Harn.

No Gloucester painting turned up for Thomas Cole, Asher B. Durand, Frederic Church, John F. Kensett, Jasper Cropsey, Sanford Gifford or George Inness. Gifford and Kensett came closest: both painted the Manchester and Beverly shore just west of Gloucester, in pictures now privately held.

Beyond the list, the record is rich. Fitz Henry Lane painted his native harbor for two decades. Hunt, Bricher, Twachtman, Metcalf, Duveneck, Beaux, Prendergast and many others followed, then Hopper, Sloan, Davis, Hartley and Avery, and the Rocky Neck and Rockport colonies. This brief catalogues 113 of their Gloucester and Cape Ann works in 48 public collections.

Four points to check before relying on this

  1. Hassam's Gloucester Harbor (c. 1899). Secondary records place it at the Norton Museum of Art (53.77); the Norton's own page was not reached. A Montgomery Museum record of the same title has no confirmed artist.23
  2. Homer's Breezing Up. The NGA page does not mention Gloucester or show an accession number, though its own image file is named with the widely cited 1943.13.1.4
  3. A Homer at Smith College. A secondary list gives Smith a Shipyard at Gloucester dated 1871, two years before Homer's first documented Gloucester summer.5
  4. A Twachtman that isn't Gloucester. The Detroit Institute of Arts pastel titled Gloucester Harbor is a Newport view of 1889, according to the Twachtman catalogue raisonné, so it is left out.6
The Hudson River painters

Seven names, no Gloucester picture

Museum searches, catalogue and auction records, and the Cooper Hewitt's Church drawings turned up no painting or sketch of Gloucester by Cole, Durand, Church, Kensett, Cropsey, Gifford or Inness. Their coastal subjects cluster at Newport, Beverly, Long Island Sound, Grand Manan and Mount Desert.78 A Cape Ann Historical Museum exhibition naming the region's painters lists Lane, Homer and Hunt, and none of the seven.9

Gifford came closest. He painted the beach at Manchester-by-the-Sea, which borders Gloucester, twice: Manchester, Massachusetts (1864), offered by Questroyal Fine Art,10 and A Sketch at Manchester, Massachusetts (1865, Memorial Catalogue no. 397), sold at Christie's in 2013.11 Both are privately held. Whether they count as Cape Ann depends on how broadly the region is drawn.

Kensett worked the same shore. His 1868 beach view at Eagle Rock has been sold with “Magnolia” in its title (Magnolia is a village of Gloucester), but earlier records call the spot Manchester.12 His A Quiet Day on the Beverly Shore (1871) gained “Magnolia” in its title only later.13 Neither is in a museum. One attribution needs correcting: Eagle Head, Manchester, Massachusetts is by Winslow Homer, not Kensett.14

A false lead for Church. His 1859 drawing Port of Cape Ann at the Cooper Hewitt shows Cape Breton Island, Canada, not Massachusetts.15 Martin Johnson Heade painted the marshes just north of Annisquam, but not Gloucester itself.16

In Gloucester: native; worked there 1840s–1865

Fitz Henry Lane

1804–1865

Born in Gloucester in 1804, Lane painted its harbor, Ten Pound Island, Stage Fort, Eastern Point and Norman's Woe for two decades. The Cape Ann Museum holds the largest group of his pictures; it has no stable object pages, so its holdings are cited through its own catalogue raisonné, Fitz Henry Lane Online.17 The NGA's 2025.89 numbers mark a recent gift, and its links may change.

In Gloucester: summers of 1873 and 1880

Winslow Homer

1836–1910

Homer spent summer 1873 in Gloucester at the Atlantic House, and summer 1880 on Ten Pound Island with the lighthouse keeper, Oliver Merrill. He did not work there again (MFA Boston, “When Homer Went to Gloucester”).1 The NGA's Gallery M68 hangs “Dad's Coming!” and Breezing Up side by side. His 1880 Eastern Point Light is Gloucester's lighthouse; his better-known Eastern Point of 1900 is Prouts Neck, Maine.

Before you go

Tides

Gloucester Harbor (NOAA station 8441841) has a mean range of 8.8 feet, and nearly 9.6 feet on the larger daily tides.134 Coves like Smith Cove and Wonson's Cove look completely different at high and low water, so check the tide table and plan each session around it.

Parking

From Memorial Day weekend to Labor Day, non-residents must reserve beach parking in advance through Blinkay, up to 10 days ahead. In 2026 Stage Fort Park cost $30 on weekdays and $35 on weekends and holidays.135

Painting with others

The Rocky Neck Art Colony's Art Trail marks the historic painting sites of East Gloucester.136 Cape Ann Plein Air runs each autumn across Gloucester, Rockport, Essex, Manchester and Ipswich; the 2026 event was October 3–11.137

Private land

Several historic vantage points, Banner Hill among them, are now private.138 Paint from roads, public ways and beaches, and ask before setting up on anyone's land.

Where the sun rises and sets

Most of these pictures were painted in summer. The sun rises and sets well north of east and west in June, and moves south through the autumn. Compass directions are from true north.

Reading the light in the paintings

  • Lane favored late-afternoon and sunset light, with the sun low in the west, and worked from measured drawings made on the spot.139140
  • Metcalf and the Banner Hill impressionists worked in the morning, with the sun behind them and the town lit full-face.141
  • Homer in 1880 painted sunsets and moonlight from the middle of the harbor.142143

Where they stood

Lane's catalogue raisonné identifies the foreground of Ten Pound Island, Gloucester as George Rogers's wharf on Fort Point, with the island at center and Eastern Point beyond; Erik Ronnberg aligned the viewpoint on the 1865 Harbor Commissioners' chart.139 For Ship in Fog the catalogue puts Lane in the channel “just off Fort Point.”144 The 1847 Thyssen picture was taken from a little higher and farther back, near Duncan's Point.145

The light

The catalogue calls the light of Ten Pound Island “the hazy light of a late summer afternoon,” in apricot hues.139 Ship in Fog is early morning, the summer sea fog burning off in stages.144

Paint it today

  • The island and harbor mouth are unchanged in outline, but Fort Point's 1850s wharves are gone, and the tower Lane painted (1821) was replaced in 1881.146
  • We could not confirm public shore access on Fort Point itself. Ten Pound Island is also visible across the water from a public way on Rocky Neck.147
  • For the Lane effect, go in late August, about two hours before sunset; for the fog picture, a still June or July morning.

Where they stood

In summer 1880 Homer boarded on Ten Pound Island with the lighthouse keeper, Oliver Merrill.142148 Princeton places Eastern Point Light in the series “painted by Homer on Ten Pound Island.”143 Where on the island he sat is not recorded; the line on the map runs from the island's light to Eastern Point Light, almost due south.

The light

Eastern Point Light is a moonlit night: rapid blue washes for the sky, the full moon “scraped out,” the water dappled by gouging with the brush end.143 Most of the series are sunsets over the western shore;147 in early August the sun sets in the west-northwest, to the painter's right when facing the harbor mouth.

Paint it today

  • The island is owned by the City of Gloucester, which keeps walking paths to the light; private boaters may land, but there is no landing facility. The tower itself is Coast Guard property and closed.148
  • Without a boat, paint the island and Homer's sunsets from the Rocky Neck public way, looking west across the harbor.147
  • Homer's 1880 method, per the MFA: let paint soak into the paper, blot it out, then lay on thick opaque dashes.142

Where they stood

Banner Hill is the ridge above Smith Cove, from which “many of the most notable American impressionist landscapes of Gloucester were painted.”141 Metcalf's Gloucester Harbor was “painted from Banner Hill looking westward”; Hassam's 1899 view was taken from the same hill, “a little farther to the left” than Duveneck's.141138 The marker is the hill's road (OpenStreetMap); the exact spots are not recorded.

The light

Metcalf painted “with the morning sun at the artist's back,” so the town across the harbor is lit full-face.141 At 9 a.m. in early August the sun stands in the east, about 36° up, behind and to the right of a painter facing downtown.

Paint it today

  • The Banner Hill vantage is now private property and not open to the public; the view can be seen from the road.138
  • Go in the morning. In the afternoon the town is backlit and the harbor turns to glare.
  • Rocky Neck's Art Trail marks this and other historic painting sites.136

Duveneck painted the same rock about 1893.150 From the marker the rock lies south-southeast; because the Terra Foundation describes Lane's view as looking east across the cove, he may have stood farther west along the shore.

The light

The Terra Foundation describes the view as looking east across the cove, “the unseen sun in the west” tinting the far rocks pink while the foreground lies in shadow: late afternoon.151 One version shows autumn foliage, so Lane varied the season.151

Paint it today

  • In Lane's day the cove was part of the private Niles Farm, with Niles Pond between it and the harbor.149 We could not verify public shore access today.
  • Go late in the afternoon with the sun behind you, and watch the far rocks turn warm in the last hour.
  • Lane worked from a measured drawing, then painted several near-identical canvases from it. Make the drawing on site and the painting at home.
