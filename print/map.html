Stand where the painters stood

Painted Gloucester · a pocket chart of nine places on Cape Ann where Lane, Homer, Hassam and others set up their easels.

  1. 1

    Fort Point, looking at Ten Pound Island

    Fitz Henry Lane: Ten Pound Island, Gloucester, 1850s

    Stand Fort Point shore access is unconfirmed. The same view of Ten Pound Island is open from the public way on Rocky Neck.

    Light Late August, about two hours before sunset.

  2. 2

    Ten Pound Island, looking out the harbor

    Winslow Homer: Eastern Point Light, 1880

    Stand The island is reached only by boat. Without one, paint it from the Rocky Neck public way, looking west.

    Light Sunset.

  3. 3

    Banner Hill, above Smith Cove

    Willard Metcalf and Childe Hassam: Gloucester Harbor, 1895

    Stand The hilltop vantage is now private. Look from the road.

    Light Morning. By afternoon the town is backlit.

  4. 4

    Brace's Cove, Eastern Point

    Fitz Henry Lane: Brace's Rock, Eastern Point, Gloucester, c. 1864

    Stand Public shore access is unverified. Check on site before you set up.

    Light Late afternoon, sun behind you.

  5. 5

    Norman's Woe, the western shore

    Fitz Henry Lane: The Western Shore with Norman's Woe, 1862

    Stand Shore north of the rock. Sanctuary rules unverified; mind the tide (about 9 feet).

    Light The last half hour before sunset.

  1. 6

    The Loaf, looking along Coffin's Beach

    Fitz Henry Lane: View of Coffin's Beach, 1862

    Stand The Loaf is mapped as a nature reserve; access to Coffin’s Beach is unverified.

    Light Sunrise. Be in place before it.

  2. 7

    Eastern Point, looking back at the city

    Frank Duveneck: Horizon at Gloucester, c. 1905

    Stand By Eastern Point Light (closed) and the Dog Bar Breakwater. Stay off it in breaking waves.

    Light Low tide, about two hours before sunset.

  3. 8

    Annisquam Light, at the river mouth

    H. Bolton Jones: Lighthouse Beach, Annisquam, ca. 1887

    Stand From Wingaersheek Beach. Summer parking must be reserved.

    Light Morning, around 9 a.m.

  4. 9

    Wonson's Cove, Twachtman's last canvas

    John H. Twachtman: his last canvas, painted at the Harbor View Hotel (Nelson-Atkins Museum)

    Stand Rocky Neck Art Trail stop 15, on foot from Rocky Neck Avenue.

    Light About two hours before sunset.
