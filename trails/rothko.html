Trail · East Gloucester

Rothko, Avery and Gottlieb

In 1932 Milton Avery invited two young New York painters, Mark Rothko and Adolph Gottlieb, to Gloucester. They drew the bathers on the beaches and the wharfs across the harbor. Six stops, from the beach to the fish flakes.

The three painters’ summers here are the subject of Avery, Gottlieb & Rothko: By the Sea, shown at the Cape Ann Museum in 20261 and at the Phillips Collection in Washington from October 24, 2026, to January 24, 20272. Rothko and Gottlieb rented cheap rooms and kept apart from the art colony’s social life, so few addresses survive3.

Stops
6
Time
Half a day. Stops 3–5 are a short walk.
Start
Cape Ann Museum, 27 Pleasant Street

1 stop · 1 marker approximate · walking route, in order (follow the streets)

  1. By car
  2. 1

    Cape Ann Museum

    Rothko’s wharf · 27 Pleasant Street

    In August 2026 Christopher Rothko gave the museum his father’s Untitled (wharf, Gloucester, Massachusetts) of 1934, the first Rothko in its collection4. It shows the salt-fish sheds of the Reed and Gamage wharfs near Smith Cove, painted from Banner Hill (stop 5)5.

    • At the museum
      Untitled (wharf, Gloucester, Massachusetts)Mark Rothko, 1934Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester

    Directions ↗

  3. 2

    Good Harbor Beach

    Bathers and handball · Thatcher Road

    Avery painted the crowd watching the total solar eclipse of 1932 at Good Harbor1. Two years later the friends met here daily: Edith Rothko remembered that they would “go swimming and play handball,” and Rothko painted the game6. A sketch in his notebook shows the footbridge at Bass Rocks by Good Harbor Beach, a subject Leon Kroll had painted before him7.

    Directions ↗

  4. On foot · about 0.5 km (0.5 mi) of streets
  5. 3

    Avery’s studio

    Milton Avery (1885–1965) · 77–78 Rocky Neck Avenue

    Avery first summered in Gloucester in 1921; in 1926 he and the sculptor Leonard Craske rented cottages here, the one record of where he stayed3. His Gloucester Dawn of about 1921 is at Vassar8.

    • At the museum
      Gloucester DawnMilton Avery, c. 1921Frances Lehman Loeb Art Center, Vassar College

    Directions ↗

  6. 4

    Smith Cove

    The wharfs · Rocky Neck causeway · marker approximate

    Smith Cove was the center of Gloucester’s gill-net fishing. Rothko painted its wharfs again and again in 1934: the Reed and Gamage salt-fish sheds with a water tank on the roof, and rows of fish flakes covered with white cloths to keep the sun off the fillets9,10; fishermen among the gill-net reels on a wharf11; and a schooner at a wharf with its sails lowered to dry, perhaps with City Hall on the skyline12.

    • At the museum
      Untitled (wharf, Gloucester, Massachusetts)Mark Rothko, 1934National Gallery of Art, Washington

    Directions ↗

  7. 5

    Banner Hill

    The high view · above 186–220 East Main Street · marker approximate

    The ridge above Smith Cove, where the Impressionists painted (site 3)13. Rothko painted the Reed and Gamage wharfs from Banner Hill, and Gottlieb sketched them from a similar spot, possibly beside him5. The hilltop is private; look from the road.

    Directions ↗

  8. By car
  9. 6

    Calder Street

    Rothko, summer 1934 · Calder Street, East Gloucester · marker approximate

    In the summer of 1934 Rothko and his wife, Edith Sachar, rented a house on Calder Street, about half a mile from the wharfs, while the Averys and the Gottliebs summered nearby14. The Rothkos arrived first and took the house the Gottliebs had wanted15. The house number is not recorded; this is a quiet residential street.

1945: Rothko with Aaron Siskind

Rothko came back in 1945 and painted watercolors while staying with the photographer Aaron Siskind in East Gloucester, in the studio Gottlieb had found for Siskind the year before17. See the photographers.

Gottlieb’s boxes

Some of Gottlieb’s Cape Ann pictures show dead fish he gathered on the beach and set in boxes1. His Harbor Scene of about 1934 is in the guide.

  • At the museum
    Harbor Scene (Gloucester?)Adolph Gottlieb, about 1934Museum of Fine Arts, Boston

Avery at Eastern Point, 1943

A sketchbook Avery used about 1943 is inscribed for the Mother Ann Cottage on Eastern Point; it is at the National Gallery of Art18.
