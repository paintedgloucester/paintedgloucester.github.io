Women of Cape Ann

Ten stops for the women who painted Gloucester, from Fitz Henry Lane’s circle in the 1850s to Nell Blaine’s harbor in the 1950s. Most stops are outdoors and free; the museum stop is the one place to see several of their paintings at once.

In 2024 the Cape Ann Museum gathered this history in Women Artists on Cape Ann, 1870–1970, curated by Martha Oaks1. This trail follows the places that can still be visited. Several houses on it are private homes: view them from the street and keep the sidewalk clear.

Stops
10
Time
A full day. Stops 4–7 are a short walk on Rocky Neck; drive between the rest.
Start
Stage Fort Park, Hough Avenue, Gloucester

The map needs JavaScript. Every stop below has an address and a directions link.

1 stop · 1 marker approximate · walking route, in order (follow the streets)

  1. By car
  2. 1

    Stage Fort Park

    Mary Blood Mellen (1819–1886) · Hough Avenue, Gloucester

    A minister’s wife from Sterling, Massachusetts, Mellen came into Fitz Henry Lane’s world after her brother-in-law took the Universalist pulpit in Gloucester in 1855. By the middle of the decade she was working beside Lane, with free use of his drawings and at times his canvases; one painting, Coast of Maine, carries both their signatures2. Her Field Beach, Stage Fort Park, about 1850, shows this shore and is at the Cape Ann Museum2; Bowdoin owns her Gloucester Harbor (Laying Nets)3.

    • At the museum
      Field Beach, Stage Fort ParkMary Blood Mellen, c. 1850Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester
    • Gloucester Harbor (Laying Nets)Mary Blood Mellen, about 1870Bowdoin College Museum of Art, Brunswick, Maine

    Directions ↗

  3. 2

    Cape Ann Museum

    The collection · 27 Pleasant Street, Gloucester

    The museum owns Theresa Bernstein’s Gloucester in Blue (1925)4, Nell Blaine’s Wharf Studio, Gloucester (1958)5 and the archive of the Folly Cove Designers6; the Rocky Neck Art Colony also credits it with Martha Walter’s Rocky Neck Store and My Studio (1920)7. Ask at the desk which are on view.

    • At the museum
      Gloucester in BlueTheresa Bernstein, 1925Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester
    • At the museum
      Wharf Studio, GloucesterNell Blaine, 1958Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester

    Directions ↗

  4. 3

    Our Lady of Good Voyage

    Josephine Nivison (1883–1968) · 142 Prospect Street, Gloucester

    In the summer of 1923 Jo Nivison painted Church Towers, Gloucester, a head-on view of this church’s twin towers. It was the summer she and Edward Hopper painted together here, and she encouraged him to take up watercolor8. We have not found the painting in a public collection.

    Directions ↗

  5. On foot · about 1.5 km (1.0 mi) of streets
  6. 4

    Rocky Neck Guest House

    Theresa Bernstein (1890–2002) · 1 Eastern Point Road, Gloucester

    Bernstein first came to Gloucester in 1916. She and the painter William Meyerowitz stayed here after their marriage in 1919, and in 1924 bought a house on Mount Pleasant Avenue in East Gloucester (a private home), where they hosted friends including Milton Avery. Both helped found the North Shore Arts Association9.

    Directions ↗

  7. 5

    Martha Walter’s studio

    Martha Walter (1875–1976) · 37 Rocky Neck Avenue, Gloucester

    A Philadelphia painter trained under William Merritt Chase, Walter had joined the Gloucester colony by 1913 and kept a studio and gallery on Rocky Neck from the later 1910s into the 1920s. The building is now a gallery7.

    Directions ↗

  8. 6

    Sally Michel’s studio

    Sally Michel Avery (1902–2003) · 77–78 Rocky Neck Avenue, Gloucester

    In 1924 Sally Michel rented the studio next to Milton Avery’s here. They married in 1926, and her work supported the family while he painted10.

    Directions ↗

  9. 7

    Rocky Neck wharves

    Nell Blaine (1922–1996) · Rocky Neck Avenue, Gloucester · marker approximate

    Blaine rented studios on Rocky Neck in 1954 and 19585; the Whitney’s Harbor and Green Cloth, II dates from the second summer11. From 1974 until her death she summered in East Gloucester12. The marker is placed on the avenue; the studio addresses are not published.

    • At the museum
      Harbor and Green Cloth, IINell Blaine, 1958Whitney Museum of American Art, New York

    Directions ↗

  10. By car
  11. 8

    Bass Rocks

    Martha Walter · Atlantic Road, Gloucester

    Walter’s entries at the Pennsylvania Academy in 1915 included a crowded beach at Bass Rocks; Philadelphia critics praised the Gloucester picture7. Parking along Atlantic Road is limited.

    Directions ↗

  12. 9

    Good Harbor Beach

    Theresa Bernstein · Thatcher Road, Gloucester

    Bernstein was still painting this beach around 1980, when she was about ninety: the Brooklyn Museum owns Rabbits, Good Harbor Beach, Gloucester13. Summer parking is by fee.

    • At the museum
      Rabbits, Good Harbor Beach, GloucesterTheresa Bernstein, about 1980Brooklyn Museum, New York

    Directions ↗

  13. 10

    Folly Cove, Lanesville

    Virginia Lee Burton (1909–1968) · Washington Street (Route 127), Lanesville

    The children’s author of Mike Mulligan and His Steam Shovel taught block printing to her neighbors here and in 1941 founded the Folly Cove Designers, who printed textiles by hand until 196914,15. Their blocks, fabrics and records are at the Cape Ann Museum6.

    Directions ↗

Cecilia Beaux at Green Alley

The portrait painter Cecilia Beaux (1855–1942) summered at Green Alley, her house on Eastern Point, from 1906 until her death16,17. The road is private, so the house is not a stop. Her Half-Tide, Annisquam River is in the guide.

  • Half-Tide, Annisquam RiverCecilia Beaux, ca. 1905High Museum of Art, Atlanta

Jane Peterson’s streets

Jane Peterson (1876–1965) painted Gloucester’s streets and wharves in bright, broken color in the 1910s and 1920s18. The RISD Museum owns her Gloucester Street Scene19.

  • At the museum
    Gloucester Street SceneJane Peterson, ca. 1920RISD Museum, Providence
