Trail · a downtown walk, then a drive

Edward Hopper’s Gloucester

Hopper came to Gloucester in 1912 with his friend Leon Kroll, and again in the summers of 1923, 1924, 1926 and 1928, mostly painting houses in watercolor. Twelve of his subjects can still be found. The downtown walk passes eight of them and ends at the train station.

The summers are the Cape Ann Museum’s count1; the oil Sun on Prospect Street is dated 1934. Street addresses follow Catherine Ryan’s catalogue Edward Hopper All Around Gloucester2, with the museum records for each painting. The Cape Ann Museum also runs docent-led “Hopper’s Houses” walks from Pleasant Street3. Nearly every house is a private home: look from the sidewalk.

Time
Walk: about 2 hours with stops. Drive: about 2 hours for stops 9–12.
