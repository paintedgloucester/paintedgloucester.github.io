The working waterfront

The harbor in the paintings was a factory. This page explains what the painters were looking at — fish flakes, dories, schooners, nets — and where the working waterfront can still be seen.

What is in the paintings

Fish flakes

Low wooden racks on which split, salted cod was laid out in the sun to dry. Whole fields of them appear in Lane’s and later paintings of the waterfront1,2.

  • At the museum
    Untitled (wharf, Gloucester, Massachusetts)Mark Rothko, 1934Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester
Gill nets

Nets hung like a curtain in the water, catching fish by the gills. Mellen’s Gloucester Harbor (Laying Nets) shows crews setting them3.

  • At the museum
    Gloucester Harbor (Laying Nets)Mary Blood Mellen, about 1870Bowdoin College Museum of Art, Brunswick, Maine
Dories

Small flat-bottomed boats carried on the schooners and rowed out to set lines and nets; Gordon Parks photographed Gloucester crews laying nets by hand from them in 19434. Homer drew boys in dories in 1873.

  • Boys in a DoryWinslow Homer, 1873The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
  • The Green DoryWinslow Homer, 1880Museum of Fine Arts, Boston
Schooners

The fore-and-aft rigged vessels of the Gloucester fleet that brought fish home from the banks2.

  • Gloucester Schooners and WaterboatWinslow Homer, 1880Philadelphia Museum of Art
  • Gloucester HarborFitz Henry Lane, 1852Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester
Draggers

Motor trawlers that drag a net along the bottom; Emile Gruppé painted them at the wharves.

  • At the museum
    Gloucester DraggersEmile Gruppé, n.d. (artist list gives ca. 1942)New Britain Museum of American Art
Drydock

A berth where a vessel is taken out of the water for repair. Hassam painted one in the 1890s.

  • Drydock, GloucesterChilde Hassam, c. 1890sThe Phillips Collection, Washington

Where to see it today

The map needs JavaScript. Every stop below has an address and a directions link.

  1. 1

    Fishermen’s Memorial

    Stacy Boulevard at Babson Court

    Leonard Craske’s Man at the Wheel, dedicated August 23, 19255. The plaques list 5,368 men known to have been lost at sea; a single storm in 1862 took 120 men, and storms in 1879 took 1596.

    Directions ↗

  2. 2

    Salting Fish marker

    25 Rogers Street, at Porter Street

    At its peak Gorton’s ran dozens of wharves and many flake yards, where cod from the North Atlantic banks was split, salted and dried2.

    Directions ↗

  3. 3

    Maritime Gloucester

    Harbor Loop

    The harbor’s museum of its own working history, on the inner harbor7.

    Directions ↗

  4. 4

    The Fort

    Fort Square

    The point where Lane painted Ten Pound Island (site 1) and Hopper painted the fishermen’s houses of the Italian Quarter8.

    Directions ↗

  5. 5

    Smith Cove

    East Gloucester

    Rothko’s 1934 wharfs had fish flakes on the pier, the last generation of the racks Lane had painted eighty years before9.

    Directions ↗

The documentary photographs

The working harbor was photographed for the federal government twice: by Arthur Rothstein in 193710 and by Gordon Parks, who went to sea on the Alden in 19434. Those photographs are on the photographers page.
